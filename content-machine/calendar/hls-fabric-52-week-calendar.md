# Microsoft Fabric for HLS: Full 52-Week Content Calendar

## Positioning

Build trustworthy healthcare and life sciences analytics with Microsoft Fabric—from FHIR ingestion to production monitoring, research datasets, and governed AI.

Primary audience: data engineers, analytics engineers, BI developers, solution architects, research informatics teams, and implementation consultants.

## Schedule and Mix

- Week of October 26, 2026, through week of October 18, 2027.
- 32 evergreen lessons (E).
- 12 trend-responsive slots (T).
- 8 applied labs or audience clinics (A).
- Dates indicate week starts, not mandatory publication dates.

## Evidence and Limits

This is an evidence-led plan, not a proven audience-performance forecast. Earlier community research identified general Fabric pain points, not HLS-specific demand for every topic. Industry scenarios remain editorial hypotheses until validated.

Trend slots do not predict future announcements. Select a relevant verified change 7–14 days before publication. If none is relevant, publish a current-state regression test instead.

## Weekly Publishing Package

1. One anchor tutorial, experiment, or teardown.
2. One written walkthrough or newsletter.
3. One LinkedIn lesson or diagram.
4. Two standalone short clips.
5. One practical resource listed below.

## Production Standards

- Use synthetic or explicitly approved data.
- Verify current capabilities, availability, versions, permissions, and prerequisites.
- Disclose test conditions and distinguish lab results from general claims.
- Do not imply regulatory compliance certification, clinical validation, or autonomous medical advice.
- Seek expert review for privacy, legal, clinical, or regulated-data claims.
- Make metric definitions explicit; examples are not universal clinical or contractual standards.
- Show the question, data, implementation, test, limitations, and resource.

## Trend Episode Template

What changed → who it affects → prerequisites → reproducible test → limitations → adopt, wait, or ignore.

State the date tested, availability status, relevant SKU/region/permissions, what the demonstration establishes, and what remains untested.

## Validation and Reviews

Review after weeks 4, 8, and 13; revise the remaining backlog based on evidence.

- Discovery: impressions, search queries, click-through rate, new viewers.
- Learning: retention, useful comments, repeat viewers, follow-up questions.
- Qualified interest: resource downloads, newsletter signups, detailed HLS submissions.
- Segment responses by provider, payer, research, and shared-platform roles where explicitly stated.
- Do not count repeated comments from one person as independent demand.
- Establish a channel baseline before setting performance thresholds.

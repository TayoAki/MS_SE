# Proven formats: what we copy before we write a word

Every video starts from **two kinds of proof**:
- **Topic proof:** this question already won in our niche (`recreate-list.md`).
- **Format proof:** this structure already won in short-form data content (below).

The script copies the format's **shape**: the beats, the devices and the hook pattern. It never copies the words,
footage or audio. Each SCRIPT.md names its format and the winner it is modelled on, with that winner's numbers.

**Where the proof comes from.** On 2026-10-22 we listed the Shorts of 17 data, BI and tech channels on YouTube. For each
Short we divided its views by the median views of that channel's Shorts (×). 116 Shorts beat their channel by 3× or more.
We looked at the frames of the top 52 by ×, after dropping off-topic ones: the first frame plus YouTube's frames at about
25, 50 and 75 % of the video. Full tables, links and the method are in `docs/reels/shorts-formats-research.md`.

**Our own niche sets a low bar.** Fabric and Power BI Shorts are mostly studio talking heads, or trailers for long
videos ("watch the full video, link below"). Median views per Short:
| Channel | Median views |
|---|---|
| Guy in a Cube | 4,050 |
| SQLBI | 2,600 |
| Pragmatic Works | 1,300 |
| How to Power BI | 12,000 |

The formats below come from the wider data niche, where the same kind of lesson reaches hundreds of thousands to
millions of views. Bringing them to Fabric for health systems is our edge.

## The six formats (each won on at least two channels)

### F1 · "Don't start with X" (the warning)
**Winners:**
- Alex The Analyst, "Don't jump into Power BI first": 740K views, 38.9×
- Data with Baraa, "Don't Become a Data Analyst!": 476K, 26.4×
- Data with Baraa, "Don't Become a Data Engineer!": 172K, 9.6×
- Leila Gharani opens 4 of her top Shorts on a "DON'T DO THIS" sticker: 77×, 25.7×, 13.3× and 5.4×

**Beats:**
1. The warning, aimed at what the viewer is about to do. Held on screen as the title.
2. Why everyone does it: the shiny thing.
3. The trap, made concrete with one number.
4. "Here's the order that works": #1 → #2 → #3, one card each.
5. The rule in four words, then the call to action.

**Hook pattern:** "Don't [the popular move] before you [the boring fix]." This is HOOKS.md shape 8, "Stop doing X".

**For us:**
- Don't start with the AI agent (built: RC3 v2).
- Don't migrate a report before you count its rows.
- Don't buy F64 for one dashboard.

### F2 · The numbered list (3 ways, beginner to pro, or a ranking)
**Winners:**
- Alex The Analyst, "Top 5 Things You'll Learn…": 3.5M, 184×
- MyOnlineTrainingHub, "3 Ways to Sum Data | Beginner to Advanced": 3.5M, 103×
- Data with Baraa, "Learn Python… in 3 Steps": 359K, 19.9×. His roadmaps also hit 12.3×, 9.0× and 8.2×.
- Leila Gharani, "TOP 10 Excel Shortcuts in 1 Minute": 1M, 13.5×
- Kenji Explains, "3 Forecasting Methods": 250K, 12.5×. Also "Remove Duplicates (3 Methods)": 6.3×.
- Tina Huang's "Ranking… 1 to 10" Shorts: 20.2× and 10.8×

**Devices:**
- The number is in the title.
- One card per item, with a big number tag and 2–3 words ("#2 PYTHON"), often with an icon.
- Items run in order: beginner → pro, or worst → best. The last item is the best one.

**For us:**
- 3 ways to count patients (Script 3, already written).
- Import, DirectQuery, Direct Lake: slowest to fastest (RC2).
- Ranking Fabric features for a health system.

### F3 · X vs Y, with the title held on screen
**Winners:**
- Alex The Analyst, "Data Analyst vs Business Analyst": 215K, 11.3×, with the title held for the whole video
- Luke Barousse, "Python Vs. R": 168K, 4.7×
- In our niche: Guy in a Cube, "Is Ontology the new Semantic Model?": 13K, 3.2×

**Devices:**
- The title "X vs Y" stays on screen from start to end.
- One difference per line.
- A verdict: "X when…, Y when…".

**For us:** Fabric Apps vs Power BI (built: RC1 v2), warehouse vs lakehouse, Pro vs F64.

### F4 · Wrong way → fast way (the screen owns the frame)
**Winners:**
- Leila Gharani: "Dynamic Numbered List" 5.7M, 77×; "Delete Empty Rows" 1.9M, 25.7×; "AutoFit" 985K, 13.3×; "Don't drag for weekdays" 398K, 5.4×
- MyOnlineTrainingHub, "Split Text… Flash Fill": 631K, 18.6×
- In our niche: How to Power BI's feature demo, 71K, 5.9×. Its layout puts the screen on top and the face below.

**Devices:**
- The slow way comes first, with a "DON'T DO THIS" sticker.
- A banner names the trick.
- Key presses show as keycaps.
- A check mark lands on the result.

**Needs:** your own Fabric screen on a trial with synthetic data. Microsoft's screenshot terms apply (`systems/3-editor/BROLL.md`).

**For us:** stop refreshing models one by one, shortcut instead of copy, RC4 (the report skill test).

### F5 · The blunt question, the honest answer
**Winners:**
- Luke Barousse, "Is web scraping legal?" (answer: "Probably"): 709K, 19.7×
- Seattle Data Guy, "Is Data Engineering Still A Good Career Choice?": 115K, 12.5×. Also "The Reality Of Being A Data Engineer": 6.3×.
- In our niche:
  - SQLBI, "Honestly, DirectQuery is for the rich!": 3.8×
  - SQLBI, "Honestly, DAX UDFs are a game changer": 3.1×
  - Pragmatic Works, "Why Does Copilot Answer The Same Question Differently?": 3.2×

**Devices:**
- A close face and eye contact.
- The question is the first line, and the one-word answer comes right after.
- Then the reasons, with keyword captions.

**For us:**
- "Is Fabric HIPAA compliant? Honestly, that's the wrong question."
- RC5: why teams push back on Fabric.

### F6 · The big number, explained with a diagram
**Winners:**
- Fireship, "Why do computers suck at math?": 8.7M, 6.7×
- ByteByteGo, "1 TRILLION messages": 2.4M, 6.3×
- ByteByteGo, "How Big Tech Ships Code to Production": 1.9M, 5.0×
- ByteByteGo, "Netflix Tech Stack": 1.7M, 4.5×

**Devices:**
- The diagram owns the frame, with the presenter as a cut-out in a corner.
- The number appears in giant captions.
- The picture zooms to each part as it is named.

**Hook pattern:** one specific number plus a named system.

**For us:** "One health system: more than 12,000 reports a month" (Chartis). The Learn diagrams are the pictures.

## The craft checklist (most of the 52 winners did all of these)
1. **The first frame carries the promise** in 2–6 words, as a cover card or a held title. On LinkedIn the first frame
   is the thumbnail.
2. **Hold the title on screen** in talking-head formats. Alex The Analyst did it in three of his winners, Data with
   Baraa in one, Tina Huang in two. LinkedIn autoplays muted, so this matters more there than on YouTube.
3. **Captions are 1–4 bold words**, with the keyword in the accent colour.
4. **The picture changes every 2–3 seconds**: a card, a sticker, a screen, a cut. Keep lines to about 10 words.
5. **Numbers go on screen as cards**: "#1 ONE DEFINITION", "412 · 522 · 946".
6. **Screen or diagram on top, face below.** How to Power BI, Tina Huang, Data with Baraa, Fireship and ByteByteGo all
   use this layout. It is the Persa layout we already have.
7. **A close, expressive face.** No intro: the first word is the hook. Talk fast. The example cuts are timed at
   3 words a second.
8. **End on one ask**: our comment keyword.

## Never copy
- Film, TV or celebrity clips. Luke Barousse cuts to them; they are not ours to use.
- Other creators' memes or footage.
- Product logos as decoration. Leila Gharani uses the Excel logo; we use words.
- "Watch the full video" trailers.

## Limits
- YouTube Shorts only. TikTok and Instagram need the ScrapeCreators key, and LinkedIn can't be scraped.
- We could not measure lengths, and could not read transcripts: YouTube blocks video pages from the cloud container.
  Formats were read from titles and frames.
- View counts are as of 2026-10-22.
- Re-run the study each quarter: `.claude/skills/niche-scripts/scripts/shorts.py`.

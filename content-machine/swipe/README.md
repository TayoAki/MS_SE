# Swipe file — posts that already worked, saved by hand

The LinkedIn-strategy trick is "research before you write": look at what already landed with your audience and reuse its
STRUCTURE (hook shape, story beats, mic drop), never its words. We do it by hand, not with a LinkedIn scraper: LinkedIn's
User Agreement bans scraping tools, and your LinkedIn account is your sales channel.

## How to add one (30 seconds)
When a post or video stops you on LinkedIn (or YouTube, Reddit, a newsletter), paste into Claude:
the link, the author, the text (or the video's hook line), reactions/comments as shown, and — if you can see it — roughly
what that author's posts usually get. Say "add to swipe". Claude appends a row to `swipe.md` and templatises it.

## X factor = this post's reactions ÷ the author's usual reactions
A post with 400 reactions from someone who usually gets 40 (X factor 10) tells you more than 4,000 from someone who usually
gets 3,000 (X factor 1.3): the post itself worked, not the follower count. "Usual" = your rough read of their last ~10
posts. Anything with X factor ≥ 3 is worth templatising.

## Templatise (what Claude does with a swipe)
Keep: the hook shape, the beat order (hook → bridge → meat → mic drop → question/CTA), the length, the format (talking
head, screen demo, list). Replace: every word, every example, every number — with yours, from `story-bank.md`, the
calendar and the field guide. If you reuse someone's image idea or framework closely, credit them in the post.
